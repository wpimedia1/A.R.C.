# Mission decisions, conflicts and known limits

Package: `council-dashboard/backend/mission/packages/arcadia-mars-v1.json`
Validate and print every value: `python -m mission.package` (run from `council-dashboard/backend`).

Every number used by the mission is either **published** (source, page/slide, quoted sentence, retrieval date), **derived** (formula over other parameters, recomputed at load) or **computed** (IAPWS R10-06 / IAPWS-95 water and ice properties via the `iapws` package). The mission refuses to start while any operator decision below is unapproved.

| D16 | Measured → estimated → checked. Estimated: for every sol between the newest measurement and today, plus 3 sols ahead, each variable is estimated from the station's own history: Ls climatology (±7.5°, widened to reach 10 samples) plus the newest anomaly decayed by the series' lag-1 autocorrelation, with a 95 % interval. Checked: when the measurement arrives, every estimate for that sol is marked confirmed (inside the interval) or corrected, with the error. The director agent receives all three with their stored ids and may order protective standby (1–72 ticks) only by citing stored ids: each reference must name an existing measurement, estimate or check and a variable it carries; the stored value, record hash and the agent's interpretation are kept with the order. | Approved 2026-10-31 (owner instruction); revised 2026-11-02 (review) |

| D18 | Excavator batteries. Excavators work on their batteries (1,410 Wh; 0.761 Wh per kg excavated; Schuler et al. 2019, Table 1) at the rate that delivers the published 2.7 t/day in the 16 mining hours (Mueller et al. 2016), and recharge from the fission grid at the average power that returns one day's energy in the 8 recharge hours. They start fully charged. Work stops when a battery cannot supply the next step; recharging is decided by the agents (charge_batteries) or, in the fixed-priority benchmark, automatically. A unit does no work in a step in which it charges. Driving to the charger is not modeled. | D19 | The survey rover drives only while the sun is above the horizon at the site (sun elevation at the end of each step, Mars24). The published distance per sol (175.15 m) is kept and driven over that simulation sol's daylight steps; at night the rover waits and its job says so. Night steps are planned pauses, not shortfalls. Basis: NASA, Perseverance "needs light for taking images or driving using auto-navigation" (Newman 2022). Excavators unchanged (D18). The offline benchmark has no mission clock, so the rule is not applied there and the benchmark says so. | Approved 2026-11-02 (owner) |

## Known limits

- Readiness is a milestone, not the end: operations (added work, charging, failures, replacements) continue after it.
- Jobs and battery chargers book power on one ledger; when supply falls below booked demand, work awaiting or under way and charging are released until it fits.

- Overburden up to 20 m (Mank et al. 2021) is drilled at 0.35 mm/s (D9); drilling energy is not published.
- The fission power unit (40 kWe) is a NASA lunar demonstration design applied to Mars.
- RASSOR 2.0 "per day" is a 24 h period of 16 h mining and 8 h recharging (Mueller et al. 2016, p.3). The engine's 24 h average power (450 W) uses the same daily energy as 675 W over 16 h.
