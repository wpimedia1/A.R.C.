# Current mission decisions and modeling limits

This file reflects the current package. Historical choices remain in earlier Git revisions and mission snapshots; upgrading appends a revision rather than rewriting them.

Published parameters have source, locator and quotation. Derived values have formulas. NIST published density replaces the former IAPWS runtime calculation. Scenario allocations and initial conditions are explicitly identified; they are not measurements.

| D14_random_failures | The rover and the excavators can fail while working. The fission unit is designed for 10 years without maintenance and does not fail; the Rodwell drill has no published failure rate and does not fail (gap). A failed unit stops its job and is replaced under D12. | Owner instruction: equipment failures must be modeled from published data. |

| D18_excavator_batteries | Excavators use published RASSOR reference battery capacity and energy per kilogram, recharge from the commissioned grid and pause when energy is unavailable. Charging travel is not modeled. | Owner instruction 2026-11-02: agents keep themselves online by managing recharging. |
| D19_rover_daylight_driving | The survey rover drives only while the sun is above the horizon at the site (sun elevation at the end of the step, Mars24). The published distance per sol (rover.max_drive_m_per_sol) is kept and driven over that simulation sol's daylight steps. At night the rover waits and its job says so; night steps are planned pauses, not shortfalls. The excavators are unchanged (RASSOR 16 h mining / 8 h recharge, D18). Without a mission clock (the offline benchmark) the rule is not applied and the benchmark says so. | NASA (Perseverance): "the rover needs light for taking images or driving using auto-navigation". | D20_agent_memory | Agent memory is tiered. Working memory holds each agent's planning cycles, tool results and work outcomes for the current sol. When a sol ends, each agent consolidates its finished sols into at most 24 lessons of at most 300 characters, each citing the recorded cycle, decision or event ids it rests on; uncited lessons are refused, and the consolidated working entries leave the agent's context. The environment package and operator decisions are never part of memory and cannot be changed by the agents. The append-only mission record keeps the complete history, so every lesson can be checked against what happened. | Owner instruction 2026-11-02: long-running autonomy needs bounded memory without losing what was learned (semantic consolidation), and without drift of the core rules. |

Operational boundaries:

- Readiness verifies the configured jobs, not human safety or a NASA mission schedule. Habitat offload is excluded.
- Water feed and storage are finite scenario allocations, not a measured recoverable deposit or tank design.
- The 15 kW RedWater allocation is a whole-system design estimate; drilling-only load and transient peaks are not validated.
- The rover model accounts for reference mobility consumption and generator recharge. It does not establish total housekeeping, temperature-dependent capacity or terrain demand.
- Local component telemetry is absent. MCD air/surface climate cannot substitute for internal battery or electronics readings.
- Whole-unit replacements use an idealized Hohmann logistics model and historical cost scaling. Launch vehicle, landing capability and suppliers are not qualified.
- Earth/Moon construction packages have their own restricted study scope. Their zero booked financial entries mean finance is excluded, not free hardware.
- Operating limits are operator-specified scenario controls. No new fixed provider-cost policy was imposed by this repair.
